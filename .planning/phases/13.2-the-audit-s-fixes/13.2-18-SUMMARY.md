# Phase 13.2 Plan 18: The calendar window keeps its first day, and the Outlook read keeps moving Summary

An event made here on a Week or Month view's first day is drawn in that view, and the Outlook
calendar reads a fresh year ahead at least every 30 days, survives an expired marker, and keeps
only what the round it is reading can speak for.

## What works now

- **Finding #4 (R13), fixed.** `events_that_could_fall_between` binds the date part of `from`
  for the lower bound, through `the_day_a_window_starts`, so "2026-10-12 09:00" and the bare
  "2026-11-01" are in windows starting that day, and an event that ended on the 11th is not in
  a window starting the 12th. The upper bound and the query string are unchanged. Reached in the
  running program through the three callers in `wx_app.rs` and `managers.rs` that draw Week,
  Month and Agenda.
- **Finding #27 (R12), fixed as its verdict corrects it.** `sync_microsoft_calendar` follows a
  stored link only while `the_round_still_running` says its round began fewer than 30 days ago;
  otherwise it asks Graph for a fresh window. The window is worked out on every run from
  `HOW_FAR_BACK_THE_READ_ASKS` and `HOW_FAR_AHEAD_THE_READ_ASKS`, so the read after a 410 carries
  it. `last_full_sync` moves only when the run read a window, the 410 branch included.
- **Premise 3's two additions.** Under @removed a row is deleted only when its stored dates fall
  inside the round's window, the window around the round's start; and after a read of a window,
  `take_off_what_outlook_no_longer_holds` drops the Outlook rows inside it the answer did not
  name. That closes **#29's Outlook calendar quarter**.
- The removal pass keeps: a row with a change waiting here, a row deleted here, a row outside the
  window, a series any of whose days the answer named, a day kept out of a named series, and
  whatever the push just sent.
- Reached in the running program through `spawn_calendar_sync` in `wx_app.rs`, which calls
  `sync_microsoft_calendar`.
- PIM-06 gains a dated line and no `[D]` clause; its box stays open. #27 and #29's Outlook quarter
  sit under no requirement: none names the Outlook calendar sync.
- `cargo test --lib data::message_cache::calendar::`: 58 passed (56 before plus two).
  `cargo test --lib application::calendar::`: 204 passed (196 plus eight).
  `cargo test --lib application::caldav_sync::`: 68 passed, unchanged. Whole library: 9,217
  passed, 5 ignored.
- `git grep -n -E 'Duration::days\((180|365)\)' -- src/application/calendar.rs` names the Google
  read's two pairs (:940-941, :964-965) and the test at :10677, and no longer the Outlook pair.

Microsoft's event delta page, dated 2024-06-21 and read 2026-10-16: "Within a round of delta
function calls bound by the date range of a calendarView, you may find a delta call returning
two types of events under @removed with the reason deleted: Events that are within the date
range and that have been deleted since the previous delta call. Events that are outside the date
range and that have been added, deleted, or updated since the previous delta call. Filter the
events under @removed for the date range that your scenario requires." So the reason is
`deleted` for both, and nothing but the stored dates can tell them apart, which is what the code
now reads.

## Deviations from Plan

**1. [Rule 2] What the push has just sent counts as named.** `push_to_microsoft` now hands back
Outlook's identities for what it took, and the removal pass counts them as named, the rule
`caldav_sync` already keeps beside its own removal pass. Without it a meeting created here and
read back a moment later by an answer that does not carry it yet would be dropped in the same
pass that sent it. Held by `test_a_meeting_this_sync_sent_to_outlook_is_not_taken_off_by_its_read`
(a pin in the red, since today's code had no removal pass) and its own record.

**2. [Rule 2] A series is named through its days.** A calendar view sends the occurrences of a
series rather than the series itself, so `everything_outlook_named` adds each occurrence's
`seriesMasterId`. Without it the removal pass would drop every series made here and pushed. The
plan's "the days kept out of a series included" is carried by `cut_from_event_id` against the
named rows. A record holds the first half.

**3. [Shape] The round's window is tested through the requests and the removals, not as its own
pure function.** `the_stretch_a_round_reads` is pure and takes its moment, as the plan asks, but a
stub of it wired before the green would have changed what the first read asks, so its values are
held by the cases that read the request (`startDateTime` and `endDateTime` present) and by the
removal cases, which place rows 10 and 730 days out. `the_round_still_running` has its own pure
case with the clock passed in. The sync cases still read the clock for "40 days ago" and "5 days
ago", since the sync takes no clock.

**4. [Red] A wired stub.** The red commit carried `the_round_still_running` as a stub answering
"still running" for every round, which is what the sync did, so the build stayed clean without an
`allow`. Two cases passed against it and were committed as pins, not named: a round five days old
goes on from its link, and a meeting this sync sent stays.

**5. [Fixtures] Two existing tests adjusted in the red.** `point_the_sync_at` now stores a round
that began now, since a link with no start would no longer be followed; and the @removed case's
row is dated ten days ahead, since a removal notice outside the round's window now keeps the row.
Both passed against the old code.

**6. [Brief] A second re-measure.** The brief asks for one call naming every flagged record.
Nine came out short in it, and CLAUDE.md says a short record is corrected by hand and the run
after it records it, so a second call named those nine.
