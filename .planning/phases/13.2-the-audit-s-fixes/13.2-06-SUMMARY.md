# Phase 13.2 Plan 06: Accounts keep their rows, a taken address is refused, and watches stop Summary

Saving the accounts no longer deletes another account or resets the order; a second account on
one address is refused with a sentence in the Account Manager and again in the store, before the
credential store or a browser sign-in; and an account removed or switched off stops being
watched at once.

## Deviations from Plan

1. **[Shape] The window is shown again, not rebuilt filled in.** The plan gave `show_edit`
   and `build_account_edit_dialog` a new way to open the Add window holding an account that
   is not stored. `show_edit` instead loops on the window it already built: on a refusal it
   says the sentence and shows the same window again. That keeps every choice typed,
   including what the account may change, the Trash choice and the signature, which are not
   fields of `Account` and which a rebuilt window would have lost; it leaves the seven
   callers of `build_account_edit_dialog` alone; and the title stays Add Account or Edit
   Account because it is the same window. So the plan's case for a "filled in but new" build
   was not written; its place is taken by a reading that the editor asks before it keeps
   anything.
2. **[Rule 2] The refusal is asked inside the editor, before anything is kept.** The plan
   had the arms loop after `show_edit` returned. `show_edit` writes what the account may
   change, its Trash choice and its signature under the account's id before it returns, so a
   refusal after it would have left those settings under an id no row ever holds. The arms
   hand `refusal_for` to `show_edit` instead, and `the_account_typed` was moved out of it to
   make the loop readable.
3. **[Rule 2] An account keeping the address it already has is never refused.** The rule
   compares ignoring case, the column's UNIQUE does not, so an older database can hold one
   mailbox twice in two cases; refusing every save of either would have locked the person out
   of the Account Manager, which saves every account on every change. A case holds it.
4. **[Shape] The acceptance grep for `one_address_one_account` names the import, not the
   arms.** The arms call `refusal_for`, which asks the module; `git grep -n "refusal_for("`
   names the Add arm at `:532` and the Edit arm at `:605`.
5. **[Rule 3] A reading added for the order record.** The plan named a record whose break
   takes the reorder out of `move_the_chosen_account` and no test that would see it, so
   `account_order.rs` gained `the_window_learns_the_new_order`, a reading with a companion.
6. **[Rule 1] The watch's reading reads statements, not lines.** rustfmt broke the lookup in
   `spawn_mail_watch` over five lines in the green, and the red reading looked for one line.
   The helper now splits the function at `;`; the change is in the green commit, and its record
   was measured against the corrected reading.
7. **[Shape] `if !woken.is_empty()` rather than an early return** in the `MailboxChanged`
   arm, which sits inside `handle_update`'s match: the same effect, and no return out of the
   whole handler from one arm.
8. **[Scope] Ledger 848 opened** for a question the plan did not raise: the refusal's visible
   equivalent is the Account Manager's status line, under the window that comes back.
9. **[Process] `awk` was run once, read-only, with its output thrown away,** against the
   brief's rule that it is not run at all. Nothing was written by it.
