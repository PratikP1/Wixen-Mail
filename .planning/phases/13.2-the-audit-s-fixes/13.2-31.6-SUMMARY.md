# Phase 13.2 Plan 31.6: A feed reaches only its own calendar Summary

A subscribed feed's changed days, and a calendar server's, are now found, changed and deleted
only in the calendar being read, through a store lookup and delete that match account,
calendar and name together; and, as ledger 878 asked, a cancelled day is counted deleted once
rather than on every read.

## What changed

- `src/data/message_cache/calendar.rs`: `A_ROW_IN_ONE_CALENDAR`, beside
  `A_ROW_ONE_CALENDARS_READ_OWNS`, and `event_in_one_calendar` and
  `delete_event_in_one_calendar` through it. Two cases: a lookup finds that calendar's row and
  no other calendar's, no row in no calendar and no other account's; a delete leaves the same
  name everywhere else. `data::message_cache::calendar::` 60 passed (58 plus two).
- `src/application/caldav_sync.rs`: `one_caldav_day_kept_out_of_its_series` reads the series
  once, after the series' own deletion check, and the day's own row, both in one calendar, and
  deletes the cancelled day's row in one calendar. Both callers, `sync_caldav_calendar` and
  `refresh_subscription`, already handed it `(account_id, &calendar.id)`. **The calendar
  server's path was affected too, since `ad2b0fd3`, and is fixed in the same function**: two
  server calendars carrying one meeting no longer reach each other's copy. A series held only
  in another calendar is left as it was and the day stored as the meeting it says, cut from
  nothing. The plan's Python reading of the shipped code: 1 test module marker, 0 account-wide
  reads or deletes (four before), exit 0.
- Ledger 878: the fold now takes `HeldBeforeTheRead`, the calendar's rows as held before the
  read beside the deletion notes, and counts a cancelled day against the series held then.
- `application::caldav_sync::` 86 passed (80 plus six). Also run green on the branch:
  `application::answered_meetings::` 37, `application::calendar_conflict::` 5,
  `application::calendar_source::` 52, `application::calendar::` 215, `service::caldav::` 176,
  `house_style` 78.
