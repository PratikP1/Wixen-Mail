<objective>
Make setting a message to go at a chosen time a thing somebody can do.

Purpose: `docs/changelog.md` tells users "A message can also be set to go at a
chosen time. A time in the past is refused rather than sent immediately, and one
more than a year ahead is refused as a likely mistake." Every clause of that is
implemented and tested in `sending_later.rs`, and `schedule` has no production
caller, so the sentence describes nothing anybody can do.

This is directly behind Undo Send because they are the same machinery seen twice.
The hold and the chosen time are two variants of one stored value, read by one
function, honoured by one send loop. Plan 01 turns that machinery on. This plan
adds the second variant and the control that produces it, and that is nearly all
it is.

Output: a Send Later button in the composer, a date and a time chosen by
keyboard, a refusal with a reason where the time will not do, a message that goes
at the time it was set for, and a changelog sentence that has become true.
</objective>

**2. The words already exist, all of them, and writing new ones would be the
mistake.** `Scheduling::spoken` at `sending_later.rs:485` returns the sentence
for each of the four answers, and its doc says why: "Every refusal names the
problem and the next move, because a refusal that only says no leaves somebody
pressing the same button again." The three refusals are already written:

    AlreadyPast  -> "That time has gone. Pick a time still to come."
    TooFarAhead  -> "That is more than a year ahead. Pick a time within the next year."
    NotAMoment   -> "That is not a date and time. Pick a date and a time of day."

`SetFor(at)` goes through `set_to_send`, which is the same function
`Readiness::spoken`'s `WaitingUntil` arm uses, so what the dialog says and what
the Outbox row says come from one place. Give these a caller. Do not compose a
second phrasing beside them: this codebase keeps finding that two phrasings of
one fact drift, and `whose_mail` in `blocking.rs` carries the same note.

**3. A past time is refused, not clamped, and the contrast with plan 01 is
deliberate and worth writing down.** Plan 01 reads the hold's length through
`Hold::of_seconds`, which clamps rather than refusing, because that value comes
from a settings file that may have been hand-edited or written by an older build,
and no stored number should be able to stop the program sending mail. This value
comes from a person choosing it a moment ago in a dialog that is still open.
Refusing it and saying why is the answer, and clamping it to now would silently
send a message somebody meant to delay.

Put that contrast in a comment where the refusal is handled, because the next
reader will have just read `of_seconds`'s doc and will reasonably ask why one
clamps and the other does not.

`JUST_MISSED` at `sending_later.rs:311` is the one softening, it already exists,
and `schedule` already applies it: a minute of grace, because the picker chooses
a minute and a dialog that refuses what it offered twenty seconds ago is a dialog
nobody trusts. Do not add a second grace period on top of it.

**4. Do not reach for `wxDatePickerCtrl`, and this is not a style preference.**
`src/presentation/wx_item_form.rs`'s module doc records a real screen reader
session: `wxDatePickerCtrl` and `wxTimePickerCtrl` each wrap a single native

control's own long-standing limitation and no style flag fixes it.

What that module built instead, and what this dialog copies: a date is three real
controls, a month `Choice`, a day `SpinCtrl` and a year `SpinCtrl`; a time is two,

Seconds are left out because nothing downstream stores them.

`DateFields` at `wx_item_form.rs:122` and `TimeFields` at `140` are public, and
`clamp_day_to_month` at `802` is public. `build_date_fields` at `824` and

Do not add a kind to `crate::application::item_fields` to reuse `ask_for`. That
