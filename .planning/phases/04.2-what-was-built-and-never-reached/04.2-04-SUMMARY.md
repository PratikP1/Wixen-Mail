# Phase 4.2 Plan 04: Accepting A Meeting Puts It On The Calendar Summary

**It works, and it is proved through the path the button takes.** Accepting a
meeting now leaves it on the calendar, on the day, at the time it is at, taking
up that time according to what was said. The tests do not call
`what_the_calendar_should_hold` and read its answer. They build an `Answering`
the way pressing Accept builds one, run the filing step the window now calls,
and read the row back out of a real database on disk. What they assert is what
is stored.

**What was wrong.** `what_the_calendar_should_hold` worked out the entire
calendar entry, correctly, and had one line naming it in the whole program: its
own definition. So the reply went to the organiser, the program said the
organiser would be told, and that was true, and the meeting was simply not
there. Nothing anywhere said so in either direction, which is what made it worse
than a documented limit.

**What only a real provider settles, and nothing here settles it.** The entry is
written with `pending` set, which is the whole of how it is meant to reach the
account's calendar server, and no push path in this project has ever run against
a real account. Ledger 154.

**What only an ear settles.** The screen reader checkpoint was not attempted.
It is written up in its own section below and recorded as ledger 155.

## The path from a person pressing Accept to a row in `calendar_events`

Function by function, as the plan asked, so it can be checked rather than
believed.

1. `wx_app::answer_the_invitation` reads the invitation out of the message's
   stored parts and calls `answering::whether_it_can_be_answered`, which is the
   only thing that builds an `Answering`.
2. It builds the reply with `Answering::the_answer_to_send` and sends it with
   `wx_app::send_the_answer`, which returns `HowItWent`.
3. **New:** it calls `answered_meetings::file_the_answer(cache, &account,
   &ready, answer, &went)`, at `wx_app.rs:12100`.
4. `file_the_answer` returns immediately unless `HowItWent::Sent`.
5. It asks `MessageCache::get_event_by_provider_id(account, uid)` for the
   meeting, and `MessageCache::the_version_answered_here(row.id)` for the
   version last answered into that row.
6. It calls `Answering::what_the_calendar_should_hold(answer, already_here)`,
   which is the call that had no caller, and stops on
   `WhatChanged::NothingNew`.
7. It asks `MessageCache::ensure_default_calendar(account)` where a meeting the
   calendar has never held should go.
8. `the_row_an_answer_leaves` turns the `OnTheCalendar` and the `Invitation`
   into a `CalendarEventEntry`.
9. `MessageCache::save_calendar_event` writes it, and
   `remember_the_version_answered` records which version it was.

**The single hop no test covers is step 3**, the call itself in
`answer_the_invitation`. A `#[test]` in `wx_app.rs` costs 44 guard record
re-measurements, and the plan asked for none to be added. None was: that file
holds 196 `#[test]` before this plan and 196 after.

What makes that hop cheap to leave uncovered is that almost nothing was put in
it. The rule that an answer which never left the machine must not be filed does
**not** live at the call site: it lives inside `file_the_answer`, which is in a
file no guard record names, and
`test_an_answer_that_never_left_the_machine_is_not_filed_as_though_it_had`
drives it. The call site passes `went` through and does nothing else with it.

## What the plan got wrong, measured

### The calendar had nowhere to store the version, so `WhatChanged` could never have worked

The plan's key links name `get_event_by_provider_id` as "how a second answer
finds the first" and `what_changed` as what decides whether the arriving
invitation is newer. Both functions exist and both are correct.
`AlreadyOnTheCalendar` needs a `uid` and a `version`. **`CalendarEventEntry` has
no version column and neither has any other table.**

So the second operand of the comparison did not exist. `what_changed` takes
`Option<&AlreadyOnTheCalendar>`, which is satisfied by passing `None` for ever:
it compiles, it passes, and it silently deletes two of the three arms. The plan
required the third arm and made a threat mitigation depend on it.

**The fix is one nullable column, `answered_version`, added the additive way**
with `ensure_column_exists`, plus the line in the rebuild table that the tree's
own comment says is not optional: "this rebuild runs after the columns are
added, so a column added there and missing here is dropped again on exactly the
oldest databases, and every later read naming it fails."

**It is deliberately not a field on `CalendarEventEntry`.** That struct is
constructed at **103 places** across `src/` and `tests/`, none of which has an
opinion about which version of a meeting somebody answered, and Rust requires
struct literals to be exhaustive. One fact that one module reads and writes is
not worth 103 hand edits in files this plan does not otherwise touch. It is a
column with two accessors, `the_version_answered_here` and
`remember_the_version_answered`, and the reason is written in a doc comment
beside them rather than only here. What makes it safe is that
`save_calendar_event` names its columns in both `DO UPDATE` clauses, so a column
it does not name keeps its value across a save.

`test_a_database_written_before_this_shipped_opens_and_reads_the_new_column_as_nothing`
builds the events table as the last release wrote it, puts an event in it, opens
it with `MessageCache::new` and asserts both that the event survived and that
the new column reads as nothing.

### Premise 3 is wrong about this tree, and the tests could not tell

The plan says `save_calendar_event` is `ON CONFLICT(id) DO UPDATE`, so a second
answer minting a fresh identifier writes a second row, and calls that "the
mechanism behind CRIT-4.2-08 and the whole of it".

It has a second conflict target as well,
`ON CONFLICT(account_id, calendar_id, provider_event_id) DO UPDATE`, backed by
`UNIQUE(account_id, calendar_id, provider_event_id)` in the schema. So the row
is found either way and a duplicate cannot arise.

**Measured rather than reasoned.** The identifier lookup was replaced by
`uuid::Uuid::new_v4()` on every answer and the module was run:
**all 15 tests passed**, including
`test_answering_the_same_meeting_three_times_leaves_one_entry`, which is the
test the plan expected to catch it. That test never even reaches a second write:
two answers at one version stop at `NothingNew` first.

**So reusing the identifier is still load-bearing, for a different reason one
step downstream.** The version is written *by identity*. Written against a fresh
one, it reaches no row, the calendar goes on saying the version before last was
the one answered, and the next invitation at that version is taken for a change
and writes over whatever is there. Seeing that takes three answers, and no test
here did it.

`test_the_version_answered_is_written_against_the_row_the_calendar_already_holds`
was written afterwards for exactly that, and confirmed red against the fresh
identifier and green against the real code.

## The security argument

**`status` is not touched by the answer**, and `OnTheCalendar`'s own doc says
why in as many words: "Confirmed, tentative and cancelled belong to whoever
called the meeting; a guest answering 'I might come' has not made the meeting
tentative, and writing their answer into the organiser's field is how a calendar
comes to say the meeting itself is in doubt when only one guest is." A row the
calendar already holds keeps its own status across the answer, and a row nobody
has held before gets `confirmed`.
`test_the_meetings_own_status_is_not_the_guests_answer` holds it (T-4.2-06).

**The existing untrusted-input tests were found before the path was changed**,
as the rule requires. `invitations::tests::test_reading_a_hostile_invitation_never_panics`
and `test_a_reply_built_from_a_hostile_invitation_stays_a_calendar_document`
sweep 5,000 and 2,000 generated hostile documents, and
`answering::invitations_from_strangers::test_nothing_a_stranger_wrote_reaches_a_header_or_a_sentence_intact`
sweeps 2,000 more. All of them still pass untouched. They cover the reading and
the reply. **None of them covered a row**, because until this plan no invitation
ever became one.

What keeps the new path safe is that every field of the row comes from
`Invitation`, which is what `read_the_invitation` already produces over arriving
mail and which returns `Result`. Nothing here unwraps, and each field goes into
the column that describes it: the summary is the summary, the location is the
location, and nothing is spliced into anything else.

**T-4.2-05, an invitation choosing a `UID` that names an event already on a
calendar.** The lookup and the write are scoped to `account_id`, and
`test_a_meeting_named_after_one_on_another_accounts_calendar_leaves_that_one_alone`
drives it: an unrelated account's meeting carrying the same name is unchanged,
including its `show_as`, while the answer is filed on the account that gave it.
The other half is the `NothingNew` refusal, which is what stops an older or
equal version replacing anything, and
`test_an_invitation_older_than_the_one_answered_does_not_move_the_meeting_back`
drives that.

**T-4.2-08, a malformed invitation.** An invitation naming no end is stored with
an end rather than a blank, by the calendar standard's rule that an event with a
start time and no end ends when it starts. A blank end reaches the editor as an
empty date, which the editor refuses, so a meeting stored that way could not be
opened at all. `test_an_invitation_that_names_no_end_is_stored_with_one_rather_than_a_blank`
drives it. An invitation whose start cannot be read never becomes an
`Invitation` at all: `whether_it_can_be_answered` refuses it with
`TheInvitationDidNotRead`, so there is no answer to file and no button offered.

**T-4.2-09, an answer that never left the machine.** Covered by
`test_an_answer_that_never_left_the_machine_is_not_filed_as_though_it_had`,
and the rule is inside the filing step rather than at the call site.

**T-4.2-SC: no dependency was added.** `Cargo.toml` changed for the version bump
and nothing else, and the diff was read rather than assumed.

## The end-time rule is shared rather than copied

`caldav_sync::end_of` held the standard's rule for an event whose calendar named
no end. It took a `CalDavEvent`, which an invitation is not. Rather than write
the rule a second time, it was widened to
`the_end_a_calendar_did_not_give(starts, ends, is_all_day)` and `end_of` now
calls it. The body is unchanged. `caldav_sync`'s own doc is the argument: "thirty
field mappings written twice are thirty chances for the two to disagree about
what an event is."

## `pending`, and what would have happened without it

Quoted from the field's own doc:

> Whether this copy has a change the provider has not been told about. Set by
> every path that changes an event on this computer and cleared by the push,
> which is why it is a field rather than something worked out: the compiler
> names each of those paths, and a change that never sets it is a change that
> never leaves.

Written `false`, the accepted meeting would be correct on this computer, would
appear on the day, and would never reach the account's calendar server or any
other device the person uses. That is a quieter version of the defect this plan
closes, and it reads to the person exactly like it working.
`test_the_entry_is_marked_as_a_change_this_computer_has_made` holds it.

## Deviations from the plan

### `[Rule 2: missing critical functionality]` The stored version

**Found during:** task 1, before a line was written.

Described in full above. One nullable column, two accessors, and a line in the
rebuild table. Without it the `NothingNew` arm is unreachable, which would have
made two of `what_changed`'s three answers dead code in exactly the way this

### `[Rule 1: bug]` Three assertions expected the wrong spelling of a moment

**Found during:** the green half.

Three tests asserted `2026-03-05T09:00:00+00:00`. The reader normalises to
`2026-03-05T09:00:00Z`, which is what the calendar sync writes into the same
column. The expectations were corrected rather than the code, and a comment says
why: a column holding two spellings is a column whose comparisons are decided by
which writer got there first, which this project has already been bitten by
once. All three were red before the correction for the right reason, which is
that there was no row at all.

### Deliberate departure: a test was written after a break was measured

`test_the_version_answered_is_written_against_the_row_the_calendar_already_holds`
was written after the fresh-identifier variant was measured, not before. That is
test-after by the ordering and not by the substance: the red was observed first,
against a real variant of the code, and the test was then written to make that
red repeatable. Reported rather than left to be noticed.

## Deferred: the screen reader pass, not attempted

**This is recorded as an unrun verification, not as a result.** Manual testing
happens later. Ledger 155 holds it. What follows is written so somebody can
follow it without having read the plan.

**What you need.** A build from `main` at `efc7942` or later, an account that
can receive mail, and a real meeting invitation in the inbox. NVDA running, then
the same again with Narrator. If no account is available, the alpha's first-run
state has sending switched off, so pressing Accept explains that instead: check
that the explanation is heard and that the calendar is left alone, and record
that this was what was checked rather than the sending path.

**1. After accepting, is what happened said once or twice.**
Do it: select the message carrying the invitation, press Accept, and listen
without moving.
Listen for: the sentence "Accepted Quarterly review. Ada Lovelace will be told",
or whatever the real meeting and organiser are called.
What each outcome means: one announcement is what is intended. Two would mean
the filing step and `what_answering_did` are both speaking about one action,
which is a defect in this plan's wiring. Nothing spoken at all would mean the
announcement is being coalesced away, which is a defect in the announcement
queue rather than here.

**2. Open the calendar and go to the day.**
Do it: open the calendar and move to the day the meeting is on.
Listen for: the meeting read out, with its time, and with its time described as
taken or busy.
What each outcome means: the meeting being absent would mean this plan does not
work in the running program, which the tests say it does through storage but no
test says through the window. The meeting being present but its busy state
unspoken is an accessibility finding about the calendar view, not about this
plan: the column is written, and whether the view reads it out is a separate
question.

**3. Decline a meeting, then look at it on the calendar.**
Do it: decline an invitation, open the calendar, move to the day.
Listen for: the meeting still there, and the time read as free.
What each outcome means: this is the worst of the three and the one nobody would
notice by looking. Declining and leaving the hour booked shows somebody as busy
at a meeting they are not going to, and anybody reading their free time is told
the wrong thing. The meeting disappearing entirely would also be wrong: the
record of what was declined is deliberately kept.

**4. Press Accept by mistake, and listen to the next few seconds.**
Do it: press Accept on an invitation you did not mean to answer, and do nothing
else. Listen. Then try to take it back.
Listen for: anything spoken that says the message is being held, and anything
that names a way back.
What each outcome means, and why this question exists: **there is no confirmation
step.** Pratik decided that on 2026-09-06, knowingly and against the draft's
