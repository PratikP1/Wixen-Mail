<premise_corrections>

**1. There are two panes, so `next()` and `previous()` already return the same
answer, and the direction is unobservable everywhere except here.**
`Pane` has `Sidebar` and `List`. `previous()`'s own doc says it is "the same as
`next` while there are two panes. Written separately because they are different
questions, and a third pane would make them different answers." So `Shift+F6`
and `F6` do the same thing in the main window by construction, and no fix to the
preview changes that. What the preview offers is the one place where a third
stop exists in practice and the two keys could differ. Say this plainly in the
summary rather than letting the plan read as though it made `Shift+F6` different
from `F6` everywhere.

**2. The preview does take focus, and three places in the tree say it does
not.** `panes.rs:21-25` says "The preview is not one of these. It never takes
focus, so `F6` does not stop there." `docs/KEYBOARD_SHORTCUTS.md` says the same.
And `focus_home`'s own doc, in the same file as the handler, says "A WebView
takes focus when a document loads and does not ask first. `set_can_focus(false)`
does not stop it." The `FocusHome` enum, the cell that carries it, and the
handler that reads it exist solely to put focus back from a place two documents
say it never goes. Correct the two claims and leave `focus_home`'s alone: it is
the one that is right.

**Every `wx_app.rs` line number in this plan is stale by construction.** That
file is 27,000 lines, phase 4 is rewriting it, and the numbers here were taken
before `04-08` landed: `focus_home_cell` was read at `wx_app.rs:1287` when this

**3. `Escape` posts the same message and has no direction.** The injected
handler matches `Escape` or `F6` and posts `kind: 'leave'` for both. Adding
`back: e.shiftKey` alone would give `Shift+Escape` a direction it does not have.
Carry which key it was, or carry the direction only when the key was `F6`. Say
which was chosen and why.

**4. The script is shared with the conversation window and that surface has no
direction to use.** `wire_the_way_out` is called twice: `wx_app.rs:1284` for the
preview and `wx_app.rs:18423` for the conversation window, whose `leave` handler
calls `frame.close(false)`. Closing is closing whichever key was pressed. So the
payload gains a field one of its two consumers ignores, deliberately, and that
belongs in a comment beside the handler that ignores it rather than being left
for somebody to find and think is a bug.

doc says it is "read before anything else wherever this arrives, so a malformed
context menu payload can never swallow the one keystroke that frees somebody who
is stuck." A reader that answers a direction must still answer for a payload
that carries `kind: leave` and nothing else, and the answer for that case is a
direction rather than a refusal. Getting this wrong locks somebody in a WebView.

**6. The test being rewritten cannot see the defect and says so itself.**
`test_f6_and_shift_f6_reach_the_pane_handler` at `tests/wired.rs:855` opens with
"What this cannot see: whether focus moves. It reads the binding out of the

keys reach the pane handler, and `Shift+F6` reaches it from the menu and never

**CRIT-4.2-11 closes for the preview and for the documents.** The direction
crosses the boundary, the reader acts on it, and the three sentences saying focus
never goes there are gone.

**It does not make `Shift+F6` different from `F6` in the main window, and the
summary must say so.** With two panes, `next` and `previous` return the same
answer by construction. Somebody reading the closed criterion without that
sentence would think the whole key was fixed.

`Shift+F6` is heard as going back, or just as going somewhere. The announcement
names the pane and what is in it, and does not say a direction, which may be
right or may be the next thing to fix.

**The preview does not become a full stop on the `F6` cycle, and that is
decided.** Pratik answered it on 2026-09-06, against the draft's recommendation
of considering it separately. His reasoning, which belongs in the summary rather
than only here: with snippets and the other ways this program offers of reading
mail, the cycle does not need to visit the preview at all. Somebody working by
ear reaches a message's text without going there, so a stop that costs a keypress
on every trip round the cycle buys them nothing.

So the work is the two document corrections and keeping `Shift+F6`'s direction
when leaving the preview, and nothing else. Do not add a third `Pane` value, do
not make `F6` put focus into the preview, and do not build a softened version
that visits it under some condition. `next()` and `previous()` go on returning
the same answer in the main window, which premise 1 already says, and this
decision is the reason that stays true rather than a gap waiting to be closed.

Recorded once so the next sweep finds an answer instead of finding the question:
the cost of not doing it is that somebody who wants a caret in the preview still
gets there by accident or by clicking. That was known when the decision was
taken.
