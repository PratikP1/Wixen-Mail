**1. Widening the check is the red half, and it will report more than the three
keys already found.** `documented_combinations` at `tests/wired.rs:1067` filters
on `Ctrl+` or `Alt+`, and its own comment says why: looking for the word "Space"
in the source would match anything. That reasoning is right for `Space` and
`Home` and wrong for `F8`, which is an unambiguous string. Widening it to
function keys will find whatever else is documented and unbound or bound and
undocumented, and a new check that is red on arrival is reporting a finding
rather than failing. Fix everything it finds or record each with a reason. Do not
narrow it until it cannot see them: that is the move that disarmed
`test_no_status_page_names_a_version_the_code_does_not_ship`, which
`CLAUDE.md` carries as a worked example.

**2. The other direction has to widen too or the check stays half blind.** The
code-to-doc direction reads `wx_app.rs` alone, so keys bound in
`editor_document.rs` and `wx_compose.rs` are invisible whatever the filter does.
Both files hold real bindings: the composer's injected script and the attachment
list's `KEY_DELETE` arm. Read them as well.

**3. `Ctrl+backslash` is allow-listed and its comment is now known to be
incomplete.** `tests/wired.rs:1038` allows it with a comment saying the page
binds it and there is no wx-side binding to find. `editor_document.rs:294-303`
says more: it was measured against the running composer and does not arrive at
all, by character or by physical key, typed or injected, while two chords beside
it arrive every time. The allow-list entry should carry that, so the next reader
does not take the key for one that works.

**4. Marking a key as not arriving is not the same as removing it.** The binding
stays, because the project's own comment says it stays in case the failure is
this machine rather than every machine. So the document says both: `F8` is the
way in, and `Ctrl+backslash` is bound and has not been seen to arrive. Somebody
who reads the page and presses `Ctrl+backslash` three times and concludes the
toolbar is unreachable is the person this is for.

**5. `F6` closing the conversation window is worth a second look and this plan
does not take it.** The same page tells the reader `F6` moves to the next pane,
so somebody who learned the key elsewhere closes the window with it. Documenting
it is honest and cheap; changing it is a behaviour change that belongs with
whoever is looking at `wire_the_way_out`, which is plan 07. Document it here,
say in the summary that it does not match what the key does anywhere else, and
leave the decision named rather than taken.

**6. The scrolling module's claim can be narrowed or wired, and one of the two
cannot be checked from here.** Whether a wxWidgets list would animate at all is
a fact about a running window. If it cannot be established without running the
program, narrow the claim rather than wiring something whose effect is unknown,
and record the question. Narrowing is what you do when you cannot verify the
alternative, and it is honest either way because the checkbox label makes a
general claim today and the setting reaches one surface.

**7. The braille correction here is the honesty half only, and the durable fix
belongs to phase 6.** Speech and braille ride one `UiaRaiseNotificationEvent`
and the screen reader decides which mediums it reaches, so the two tick boxes
cannot be made independent through that call. `docs/accessibility.md:47-51`
already says so correctly. Phase 6 owns the tick boxes, the two tests whose names
promise independence, and its own success criterion 1, which asks for something
that cannot be built as written. What this plan does is stop the changelog
telling a deaf-blind user they can switch speech off and keep braille, because
that sentence is the reason the feature was said to exist and it is the one case
that does not work. Do not touch `feedback.rs`, `wx_settings.rs`'s Feedback tab,
or `accessibility.rs` here.

**8. Line numbers in `docs/changelog.md` have moved and must be re-found.**
