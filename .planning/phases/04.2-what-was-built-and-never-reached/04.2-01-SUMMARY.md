# Phase 4.2 Plan 01: Undo Send Summary

**It works.** A message you send is held for ten seconds before anything hands
it to a server, it goes on its own when the ten seconds are up without anybody
pressing anything, and `Ctrl+Shift+Z` takes it back while it waits. Before this,
Undo Send refused every single time it was pressed, for every message, because
nothing in production had ever held one.

Three things had to be true at once and none of them was:

| | Before | Now |
|---|---|---|
| A message waits | No. The hold was pinned to "as soon as possible" inside a wrapper | Yes, for the length set on the Compose tab |
| It goes on its own | No. Every flush was a person pressing something | Yes, within about a second of the hold running out |
| It can be taken back | No. `take_back` answered `TooLate` for every row | Yes, while it waits |

**What only a screen reader can settle, and nothing here settles it.** Whether
the sentence said when Send is pressed finishes in time for somebody to hear it,
decide, and press the key inside ten seconds. That is the whole argument in
`Hold::DEFAULT`'s own doc and no test can measure it. Recorded as ledger 147 and
answered at plan `04.2-04`'s checkpoint, item 4, which asks this question in the
flow it matters most in. Nothing here has met a real mail server either: ledger
148.

## The census that caught something, and what it caught

`tests/nothing_leaves_the_outbox_unasked.rs` counts every place mail is handed
to a server and asks, of each, whether a person asked or an event did. My timer
made it four and the test failed with exactly the right question.

The answer is written into the record rather than the number being bumped. This
is the first entry that is an event and not a key, and what makes it allowable
under guardrail 7 is not the prose about somebody having pressed Send: it is
that `its_moment_came` answers about an **edge** rather than a level. A row asks
for one pass, at the moment its own hold runs out, and never again. So turning
the hold on cannot make more mail leave than turning it off would, and the clock
can reach no state that pressing Send would not have reached already.

## Deviations from the plan

### `[Rule 2 — missing critical functionality]` The naive timer would have opened the database once a second forever

**Found during:** task 1, before writing the timer.

The plan said to call `flush_outbox` from the timer rather than write a second
send path, which is right and I did it. What it did not say, and what reading
the callee found, is that `flush_outbox`'s first act is
`MessageCache::new`, which runs schema initialisation and **five migration
passes**. On a one-second interval that is every migration pass, once a second,
for the life of the process. Worse, `flush_outbox` sends everything currently
eligible, so a message that failed to send stays eligible and would have been
retried against somebody's server every second, with the failure counter
climbing forever. It also sends `StatusUpdated("Outbox is empty")` when there is
nothing, which at one a second is the flooding guardrail 5 forbids.

**Fix:** a cheap question asked first, over the connection the window already
holds. `MessageCache::anything_reached_its_moment` reads two small columns for
the rows that carry a moment and answers through the new pure
`sending_later::its_moment_came`, which is edge-triggered: `since < moment <=
now`. All three problems go away at once, no parameter was added to
`flush_outbox`, and the empty-outbox chatter never fires because the flush only
happens when something really came due.

**Files:** `src/application/sending_later.rs`, `src/data/message_cache/outbox.rs`,
`src/presentation/wx_app.rs`. **Commit:** `97481a7`.

## The rewrite, and what the count check is blind to

The old `wired.rs` check read the order of two calls in `undo_send`'s source. It
was green from the day it was written against a command that refused every time
it was pressed, and its own doc admitted what it could not see: whether Undo
Send is reachable. That is the pattern this phase is about.

**The count-based staleness check cannot see a rewrite, by construction**, and
saying "no check fired" would be true and worthless. In this plan every change to
`wired.rs` was an addition, so the check did fire and eleven records were
re-measured. But the *content* of two existing checks changed
(`test_the_composers_send_passes_the_hold_to_the_queue` was strengthened in task
2 and `test_the_clock_lets_held_mail_go` in task 3) with the count unchanged, and
nothing in the commit gate can see that. Neither is named by any guard record, so
nothing is stale; that is a fact I checked rather than a silence I am reporting
as a pass.

## The setting

`undo_send_hold_seconds`, a **top-level** `AppConfig` field, not nested. Nesting
is the documented hole in both settings checks and `CLAUDE.md` names the two
settings that got past them that way.

**Both checks were red about it before they were green**, which is why the field
went in on its own commit. Quoted from the run:

> 1 setting(s) can be changed and are read by nothing, so the answer is taken and ignored:
>   undo_send_hold_seconds

> 1 setting(s) are stored and survive a restart and no screen offers any of them, so nobody using this can reach them:
>   undo_send_hold_seconds

The control is a spin box in the **Sending** section of the **Compose** tab,
under "Show preview before sending" and "Keep a copy on this computer", copying
the autosave spin box in the Drafts section of the same tab. Range 0 to 60.

Set with `set_accessible_name_and_description`, not `set_name`. As a person
hears them:

- **Name:** "Hold a message before sending for, seconds, 0 for no hold"
- **Description at ten seconds:** "Send holds the message for 10 seconds first, so you can take it back."
- **Description at nought:** "Send sends the message straight away, with no time to take it back."

That description is `what_send_does`, which existed, was tested, and had no
caller. The length is read in one place, `the_hold_in_force`, through
`Hold::of_seconds`, so a hand-edited file cannot stop the program sending mail.
A settings file that cannot be read at all falls back to the default hold rather
than to no hold, because taking somebody's only way back away is the worse
failure. The number ten is named once, in `Hold::DEFAULT`.

`test_a_stored_hold_outside_what_is_offered_is_brought_inside_it_rather_than_refused`
drives `i64::MAX`, 3600, 61, -1, `i64::MIN`, 0 and 10.

## What this plan turned up and deliberately did not close

**Setting a message to go at a chosen time is still unwired.** `schedule` has no
production caller, so `docs/changelog.md`'s sentence "A message can also be set
to go at a chosen time. A time in the past is refused rather than sent
immediately, and one more than a year ahead is refused as a likely mistake" is
still false.

**The sentence was left untouched on purpose rather than missed.** By the
decision of 2026-09-06, `04.2-02` builds scheduled send and makes the sentence
true. Moving it into Known limitations here and moving it back one plan later
would be churn in an `[Unreleased]` section that has not shipped, and a merge
hazard for nothing. `git diff 9611b70 -- docs/changelog.md` was read: the
sentence appears only as **context**, with no `+` or `-` on it. `04.2-02` is the
plan that closes it, and whoever drops or defers that plan owns moving the
sentence into Known limitations at that point.

## Findings for later plans

**A meeting reply is now held too, and nobody planned that.**
`queue_for_sending` has **two** production callers, not one: the composer at
`wx_app.rs:13845` and the meeting-reply path at `wx_app.rs:12042`, which
discards the returned `waiting_on` with `Ok(_)`. So a meeting reply now waits
ten seconds and leaves on the clock like any other message. That is the right
outcome and is exactly why this plan went first, but `04.2-03` and `04.2-04`
should know the reply they send is held, and that the sentence a person hears
after answering a meeting is whatever that path says rather than the countdown.

**`04.2-02` inherits a working call site.** `queue_outbox_message_to_go` is now
the only way into the queue and every caller says what it is waiting for, so a
chosen time has somewhere to be passed. `GoAfter::Chosen`, `Readiness::WaitingUntil`,
`set_to_send` and `Scheduling::spoken` are all still live and tested and need no
change.
