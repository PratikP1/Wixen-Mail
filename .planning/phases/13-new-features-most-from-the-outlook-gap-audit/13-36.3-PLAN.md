<objective>
An organiser's update or cancellation that names one day of a repeating
meeting changes that day and no other, per RFC 5545's RECURRENCE-ID and RFC
5546's REQUEST and CANCEL for one instance, and an update or cancellation for
every day of the series is applied as a single meeting's is.

Purpose: ledger 638. 13-13 made an organiser's update move the meeting and a
cancellation offer Remove from Calendar, and refused both for a repeating
meeting, because the message is usually about one day and the calendar's copy
is the series, so applying it would move or call off every day (13-13's
deviation 2). Pratik answered "Yes." on 2026-09-27 to the recommendation "a
plan handling one day of a series, both". This plan is the organiser's half;
13-36.4 answers one day. One executor cannot finish both halves in a sitting:
this half alone reads a property nothing here reads yet, adds two columns, a

other half changes the reply, the refusal, the filing and a defect in how a
whole series is answered (13-36.4's premise 1).

store's link from a day to its row, the seam, the sentence, the button for
one day, a new target reading raw messages through to the rows the calendar

```
git show main:src/data/message_cache/mod.rs | sed -n 3311,3319p   -> ical_uid and organiser, kept off CalendarEventEntry and written by their own statement
```

A day kept apart on a calendar server goes up as a new resource under a
fresh UID, because a calendar collection holds one resource per UID, and the
server's read then records that fresh UID in `ical_uid`. So neither
`ical_uid` nor `provider_event_id` can say which meeting and which day a row
stands for, and `provider_recurrence_id` means "a server split this out and
it still shares the series' address", which would refuse every edit of the
row and send it down the wrong path. Two new columns, `replaces_day_of` (the
meeting's UID) and `replaces_day` (the day, written on the series' clock),
kept off the struct the way `ical_uid` and `organiser` are, written by their
own statement, so no save and no sync ever clears them. A day a calendar
server itself split out is found by the compound identity its read gives it,
`{uid}:{recurrence-id}` (`caldav_sync.rs:1007`).

- **The planner's, for him to overrule:**
  - **A moved day is kept apart the calendar's own way:** an appointment of
    its own naming its series, with that day called off the series, through
    `calendar::one_day_kept_out_of_the_series`, the pair the editor's one-day
    change and both providers' reads already leave. The day goes up to a
    calendar server as the pair the editor's change sends; nothing new is
    sent anywhere.
  - **A cancelled day gets the same button,** Remove from Calendar on Alt+R,
    described as taking that day off; pressing it calls the day off through
    `calendar::one_day_called_off` and says `calendar::one_day_taken_off`'s
    words. Nothing is sent to the organiser, as for a whole meeting.
  - **Only where the calendar can carry one day on its own,** by the rule
    the editor already follows, `calendar::can_be_honoured` with
    `EditMeans::OneDay`: a calendar server and a calendar kept here. On a
    Google or Outlook calendar the kept-apart day would reach the provider as
    an extra meeting while the called-off day would not reach it at all,
    because neither is told a repeat on a change (`calendar.rs:16-25`), so
    the change is said and not applied, and the calendar is named.
  - **A message naming no day is a change to every day,** applied as decision
    16 applies a single meeting's: the series moves, carrying the invitation's
    repeat and adding its called-off days to the copy's own, or Remove from
    Calendar marks the series cancelled. 13-13's reason for refusing ("a
    message about one day of it would change every day") holds only while the
    day cannot be read.
  - **From one day onwards (RANGE=THISANDFUTURE) is said and not applied,**
    and ledgered: it changes a series from a day on, which is two series, and
    no invitation read here has carried it (no real organiser's invitation
    has been read at all, ledger 631).
  - **The day is read on the series' clock:** the RECURRENCE-ID's moment in
    its own zone (its TZID, its `Z`, or the series' zone when it names none),
    written the way the series' start is written, so it compares with the
    days the series already calls off by `with_one_more_day_called_off`'s own
    rule. A zone this computer cannot place, when it differs from the series',
    is said and not applied.
  - **A one-day message against a single appointment** applies to it only when
    the appointment is at that day's time; otherwise it is said and not
    applied, because the calendar holds the meeting as one appointment on
    another day.
