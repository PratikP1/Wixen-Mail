# Phase 13 Plan 21.1: A time read in the zone it was written in Summary

An invitation from another time zone is now said at this computer's hour. A time an
invitation names with a `TZID` is read in that zone, whether the zone database names it
(`Asia/Tokyo`, a calendar server's `/mozilla.org/.../America/New_York`) or Windows does
(`Tokyo Standard Time`, placed through Windows' own ICU), and the sentence before the body and
the three answer buttons say the hour it is here. The sentence adds the other clock once when
it differs: on this machine, "Meeting invitation: Quarterly review, 04/03/2026 at 19:00 to
20:00, which is 05/03/2026 at 09:00 to 10:00 Tokyo time, in Room 4, ...", held by the target
through `mime::parse`, the store and `for_message`. "Already on your calendar" and an
organiser's move compare instants, so a Graph copy stored in UTC and an Outlook invitation in
Pacific time for the same moment are one meeting. Nothing stored changes. The calendar's own
surfaces are 13-21.2's, and ledger 632 stays open for it.

## Deviations from Plan

**1. [Premise] 13-13 already saved the update's zone, as the plan's re-take said.** The zone
case passed before any change, so it went in as `fa158fbb`, a `test` commit saying it reads
what 13-13 built, with a comment added above the line. The break, taking the line out, was
taken by hand first: the row kept "UTC" and the case, alone in its target, went red with
`left: (Some("UTC"), "2026-03-05T10:00:00")`.

**2. [Premise] Two of the second pair's cases passed before their green.** After task 1 both
sides of the standing and of the move are worded on this computer's clock, so one instant in
two zones already worded the same. The clause is what makes the worded comparison wrong: with
it in the sentence's words, an invitation from Los Angeles never words like the Graph copy. The
cases were written in the red commit, not named, and said so; the two records whose breaks go
back to words and to stored text are what hold them.

**3. [Shape] Eight records rather than the plan's seven.** The moment break is measured twice,
against the library and against the invitation target, the tree's pattern for one break seen
from two suites.

**4. [Scope] `meeting_changes.rs` in task 1's green.** `invitations::when_it_starts` took the
zone, and its one caller is there.

**5. [Scope] `presentation/date_display.rs` not in the plan's files.** The other clock is a
clock face in another zone, and `date_display` could only word a moment on this computer's
clock. It gained `a_clock_face` and `the_clock_of`, and the private `clock` takes any
`Timelike`; no case added there, no record flagged.

**6. [Shape] One comparison, in `invitations.rs`.** The plan asked for one function both callers
share, and for `grep -c 'read_in(' meeting_changes.rs` at least 1. The shared pair,
`at_the_copys_time` and `the_same_instant`, lives in `invitations.rs` and calls `read_in`;
`meeting_changes` calls them, so its own count is 0.

**7. [Shape] What "the same time" means.** Start and end as instants, an invitation naming no
end ending where filing it would put it (`the_end_a_calendar_did_not_give`), and whole days by
their first day only, as the worded comparison did. The move's short sentence, the start alone,
is chosen when the starts are different instants.

**8. [Test] One case keeps its copy in Tokyo, not UTC.** With a copy in UTC, a break that reads
it without its zone is green on a UTC runner, so the record would be red here and green on CI.
Kept in Tokyo it is red on every machine this runs on.

**9. [Ledger] Six entries opened, not five.** The plan's own list names six: two unrun-verify
and four todo.

**10. [Wording] A zone database name is said by its place, "Tokyo time", and a Windows name
as Windows writes it, "Tokyo Standard Time".** The plan's example used the Windows name; the
target asserts each form for its own document.

**11. [Brief] The banned tools' names, twice.** Two do-nothing variable assignments, each
named with one of the two banned tools, in read-only grep commands; neither tool ran and
nothing was written. Logged as observation 0871.
