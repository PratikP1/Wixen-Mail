<decisions date="2026-09-27">
- **Pratik's, 2026-09-27, item 1:** "Yes." to "a plan handling one day of a
  series, both". 13-36.3 carries the organiser's half; this plan the answer.
- **The planner's, for him to overrule:**
  - **A one-day answer is filed the way 13-36.3 keeps a day apart,** by the
    same rule for where one day can change on its own: on a calendar kept
    here or a calendar server the day becomes its own appointment carrying the
    answer's busy, tentative or free, so a declined day is free while the
    series stays busy, as 13-11 keeps a declined meeting on the calendar
    marked free. An Accept at the series' own time is kept apart too, so the
    answer has a row to be remembered on and the buttons can say it was
    given.
  - **On a Google or Outlook calendar the answer is sent and the calendar is
    left as it was,** and the one sentence after answering says so, because a
    day kept apart there would reach the provider as an extra meeting; the
    earlier answer to that day is then not remembered, and the ledger says so.
  - **With no series on the calendar, the day is filed alone** where 13-11
    files a meeting the calendar has never held, when that calendar can take
    one day on its own; its link to the meeting and the day is 13-36.3's.
  - **A series answered keeps its repeat:** the rule and the called-off days
    come from the invitation, and a series already held keeps the days it
    already calls off, including the days 13-36.3 kept apart.
  - **A document holding the series first and a changed day after it is
    answered as the series,** which is what a reply naming no day means.
  - **From one day onwards is refused** with its own sentence, as 13-36.3
    says it and does not apply it.
  - **The reply's RECURRENCE-ID is rebuilt from the value and zone the
    invitation wrote,** not copied as a line, so nothing else a stranger put
    on it reaches the organiser.
