<objective>
Make a release that cannot produce a file it promised stop instead of publishing
what it has, and put the certificate decision on the record as a decision rather
than as a question.

Purpose: success criterion 1. Its first clause asks that the published installer
and the executable inside it both carry a valid Authenticode signature with a
timestamp countersignature, verified against the published release asset. The
signing work itself is plan 07-08; this plan is everything about criterion 1 that
does not touch a key.

The publishing half is here: a release that promises four files and can only
produce three currently publishes three and says nothing, because
`fail_on_unmatched_files` is set to `false` and no release has ever run to find
out whether the globs match at all.

**What changed since this plan was first written.** It ended in a blocking
checkpoint asking which certificate to use. That question is answered:
`.planning/decisions-2026-09-06.md` decision 6 chooses **Azure Artifact Signing**,
about $10 a month, Pratik's own name as the publisher, no hardware, and records
that the United States or Canada residence requirement is met, asked and confirmed
on 2026-09-06. The checkpoint is gone and the plan is autonomous.

**The three options that lost are not deleted with it.** They become a record of
what was considered and why each lost, in `.planning/REQUIREMENTS.md` beside
SHIP-01. A decision that shows only the chosen path cannot be told apart from an
assumption nobody examined, and the recurring cost of this one will get questioned.
That is task 2.

Output: a release that fails loudly rather than quietly, a test holding the
published names to the built ones, and a decision written down with its
alternatives and their reasons.
</objective>
