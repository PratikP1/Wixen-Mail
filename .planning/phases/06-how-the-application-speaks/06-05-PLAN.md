  <name>Checkpoint: should a reminder wait for somebody to stop typing</name>
  <decision>
    A reminder comes due while you are writing a message. Today a modal window
    opens over you and takes the keyboard. What should happen instead?
  </decision>
  <context>
    **The recommendation is a combination: say it now, open the window when
    typing stops.** That is options 1 and 4 together, and the reason is a
    finding neither the deferred item nor the research carries.

    **What the deferred item says, and it is right.** "A reminder that waits
    until somebody stops typing is a reminder that can be an hour late, and
    being told at the time is the whole point of asking to be told."

    **What nobody noticed.** The sound and the spoken sentence both happen
    inside the function that opens the window,
    `src/presentation/wx_reminder_alert.rs:61-65`, immediately before the window
    is built. So a reminder simply made to wait produces nothing at all while it
    waits. No tone, no sentence, no window. An hour of silence, not an hour of a
    tone with no words.

    And `Event::Reminder` is the one event whose written equivalent **is** the
    window. Its own doc comment says so. Every other event has a sentence this
    program says; this one has a window. Hold the window and that event has no
    written form at all, which is the rule `feedback.rs` exists to keep.

    | Option | What happens | What it costs |
    |---|---|---|
    | 1. Wait | Nothing until typing stops, then the window | Small, and the code is ready: nothing is recorded as raised until the window opens, so a held reminder is re-derived next tick. **Alone, it is an hour of silence** |
    | 2. Raise without focus | The window opens behind, focus stays in the composer | Medium and different work. For somebody moving by keyboard through a screen reader, an unfocused window is one they may never find |
    | 3. Hold briefly, then raise anyway | Wait up to some number of seconds, then take the keyboard | Medium. Steals focus mid-word eventually, which is the thing being complained about, just later |
    | **4. Say it now, open the window when typing stops** | The tone and the sentence go out at the moment it is due, on a channel that does not move focus. The window waits | Small plus one split: the sentence has to be sayable without the window, which is about ten lines. Needs a rule about whether the sentence is said again when the window finally opens |

    **What option 4 does not settle.** Whether hearing the reminder and then
    meeting its window several minutes later reads as helpful or as the same
    thing twice. That is a listening pass and it is a ledger entry whichever
    option is chosen.

    **What none of them change.** A reminder is never lost. Nothing is recorded
    as raised until the window really opens.
  </context>
  <options>
    - 1: wait for typing to stop.
    - 2: raise the window without taking focus.
    - 3: hold briefly and raise anyway.
    - 4: say it now and open the window when typing stops. Recommended, and it is 1 plus a split.
    - a combination, which is the usual answer here.
  </options>
