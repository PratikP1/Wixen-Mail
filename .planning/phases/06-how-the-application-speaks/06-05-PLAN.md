<objective>
Stop a reminder taking the keyboard away from somebody in the middle of a word,
without making them find out about it an hour later, and keep telling them
until they come to it.

one-at-a-time gate with the folders question, so neither can open over the

repeating tone, and this plan is that answer and nothing wider. The widening he
also asked for, one window for every due thing with a kind on each row, is
`06-09-PLAN.md` and depends on what this plan builds.

Output: one rule about when a modal may open, in the module that already holds
the other one; a reminder that says what is due at the time it is due; a
window held for one look and then opened; and a tone that comes back once a
minute until the window has focus, with a ceiling.

**The checkpoint below was answered on 2026-09-14, and this block records what
changed in the plan because of it.** Everything in `<premise_corrections>` was
re-taken the same day; the tasks are rewritten around the answer.

**The answer.** Option 3, widened. In Pratik's words: "3 + reminder tones
every minute until the user focuses on the reminder window which should allow
for snooze, snooze all, dismiss, and dismiss all, keeping in mind that there
may be multiple reminders in the window. Similar considerations should occur
for Calendar and task reminders. Could the same infrastructure be used?" And,
when a split was proposed: "yes. But there should be an indication that a task
is due, a calendar event is coming up, or a reminder. In other words, I'm
reconsidering a single window for multiple types."

**What this plan keeps and what went to 06-09.** This plan is option 3 with
the repeating tone: hold briefly, then raise anyway, and sound the tone once a
minute until focus reaches the window. Holding the window while still saying
the reminder at the moment it is due needs the split the old option 4
identified, the sentence and the tone sayable without the window, so that
split is built here. Everything about a list, several kinds in one window,
per-row and bulk actions, and the model that lets a fourth kind arrive later
is `06-09-PLAN.md`, which depends on this plan for the split and for the
focus rule and on nothing else in the phase.

**What "briefly" is, proposed here and recorded by the executor.** One look:
sixty seconds, the value of `HOW_OFTEN_TO_LOOK`. A reminder found due while
somebody is typing is said and sounded at that look and its window opens at
the next one, whether or not they have stopped. The reason: the sentence has
already told them, so the window's job is to take an answer rather than to
inform; a minute is the unit the whole feature counts in, because a reminder
is set to the minute and the look runs once a minute; and it costs no new
timer, since the look that finds a reminder held is the one that already
runs. A shorter hold would need `raise_what_is_due` to look at held items on
every fifty-millisecond tick rather than once a minute, which is a different
shape, and a longer one is a reminder that arrives after it stopped being
about now. The old option 3 row's cost stands and the summary has to say it:
**this steals focus mid-word eventually, which is the thing being complained
about, just later.** What it buys over raising at once is a warning and a
minute.

**The ceiling on the tone, proposed here and recorded by the executor.** Ten
tones, one a minute, then silence with the window still on screen. Guardrail
5 says feedback must be bounded, and a tone every minute until somebody comes
back from lunch is a flood in an empty room. Ten minutes is long enough to
come back from the kettle and short enough that a machine left running does
not sound all afternoon; after the tenth the window is still there, its
sentence is still on it, and the reminder is not lost. The first time focus
reaches the window the tone stops for good, and it does not resume if focus
leaves again, because somebody who has heard it and gone back to what they
were doing has chosen to.

**The rule about saying it again.** Once. The sentence and the tone go out at
the moment the reminder is found due, whether or not the window can open
then. When the window opens after a hold, nothing is announced a second time:
the sentence is the window's static text and its accessible name,
`wx_reminder_alert.rs:118-119`, and a screen reader reads a dialog's text when
focus arrives in it, which is the same sentence arriving by the channel a
window uses. Whether that is what NVDA does with this dialog is a listening
pass and a ledger entry; the rule stands either way, because the sentence is
on the window for anyone reading it. The repeating tone is a tone alone, and
its written equivalent is the window on screen. Two announcements a minute
apart would be the same fact twice to somebody who cannot skim.
</correction>
