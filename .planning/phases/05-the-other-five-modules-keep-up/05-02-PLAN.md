<objective>
Give the calendar a week and a month to show, by narrowing the window it
already reads, and make Prev and Next mean something.

Purpose: criterion 3 of phase 5 and the whole of PIM-06. Two buttons have been
disabled since they were written, with accessible names ending "not built yet",
and a settings control that offered Day, Week and Month was taken out because
none of the three could be drawn. The requirement reads like a new feature and
is not one: the event list is already loaded by a date range, the expansion is
already per day, and the rows are already sorted by moment. A week view is a
narrower range and a heading.

Output: two views that work by keyboard, a pair of buttons that move by one
period and say where they landed, and a stored default that is offered where
somebody would look for it.
</objective>

**1. The month view must be a list, not a grid, and the requirement's own
wording says so.** PIM-06's third `[D]` line is "A screen reader can work
through a view's events in date order **without the user having to reconstruct
the grid from cell labels**". A seven-by-five grid of cells is the thing that
criterion is written against. The panel already holds a virtual `ListCtrl` in
Report mode with five columns, sorted by moment
(`CalendarEventItem::every_day_shown` sorts by
`common::moment::read`), and a list read top to bottom is date order by
construction. **Build the month view as the same list over a month-wide window.**
Say in the summary what a grid would have cost and why it was refused, because
somebody will ask later and the answer should be on the record rather than
rediscovered.

**2. The event list is loaded by a date range today, and the comment in the
calendar panel says otherwise.** `src/presentation/wx_calendar_module.rs` lines
46 to 52 say "the event list is loaded by the account rather than by a date
range". Read `wx_app.rs`'s calendar arm of `load_module_data`: it calls
`CalendarEventItem::the_window_now()`, passes the pair to
`events_that_could_fall_between`, and then calls
`CalendarEventItem::every_day_shown(&showing, from, to)`. The comment is stale
and **correcting it is part of this plan**, in the same commit as the change
that makes it false in the other direction.

**3. `the_window_around` is private.** `grep -n "fn the_window_around" src/presentation/ui_types.rs`
shows `fn the_window_around(today: chrono::NaiveDate)`, with no `pub`. Its doc
comment says it was split from the clock so the arithmetic could be held to an
exact pair of dates by a test with no clock in it, which is exactly the shape
the new week and month arithmetic wants. `05-RESEARCH.md` calls it "the hook"
and does not say it is private.

**4. The window is `HOW_FAR_BACK` = 180 and `HOW_FAR_FORWARD` = 365, and those
constants belong to `application::occurrences`, not to the display.** Their doc
comments say they are the same window the syncs ask the providers for, so that
a series from a calendar server and one from Google reach as far as each other.
**Do not change them.** The agenda view keeps that window. A week or month view
is a different, narrower window for the display only, and the sync's reach is
not a display concern.

**5. The narrower window does not make the read cheaper in the way it looks
like it should.** Read the doc comment on `events_that_could_fall_between`:
it is two queries, and the second one reads every event in the account carrying
a recurrence rule, whatever the window is, because only the expansion can decide
which of them falls inside. Narrowing from eighteen months to one week narrows
the first query and not the second. Do not write a changelog entry claiming the
calendar got faster.

**6. `calendar_range_label` says raw stored dates and is what the heading uses
today.** `pub fn calendar_range_label` in `ui_types.rs` takes the rows, sorts
their `start` prefixes as text and produces `"2026-07-26 to 2026-07-28"`, or
`"No events"`. That is a machine format read aloud. The criterion asks that Prev
and Next announce "the range they moved to rather than only redrawing", so the
heading has to say a period in words. `src/presentation/date_display.rs` holds
`pub fn a_day_in_words(stored: &str, settings: DateSettings)` and
`pub fn absolute(when, settings)`; use one of those rather than formatting a
date here. Note that `date_display::ENGLISH_ONLY` already records that dates are
written in English, and that FEEDBACK-02 in phase 6 is the requirement that
changes it. Do not try to solve that here.

Note also that the heading today describes **the events that came back**, not
the period that was asked for. Those differ the moment a week is empty: the
heading would say "No events" and the person would not be told which week they
are in. The heading has to name the period.

**7. Prev and Next have different names on the two Windows accessibility
channels today, and one of them says the feature is not built.** Lines 53 to 58
of `wx_calendar_module.rs`: the labels are `"&< Prev"` and `"&Next >"`, and
`set_accessible_name` sets `"Previous period, not built yet"` and `"Next period,
not built yet"`. `CLAUDE.md` explains the split: Windows takes a native

yet". **Both channels have to be fixed, and the labels are the half that is easy
