# Phase 13.3 Plan 20: What an uninstall leaves Summary

The uninstaller no longer tells somebody their mail folder stays when it is about to remove
it, an uninstall for everybody no longer promises another account still has its copy, and the
Windows Search guide says `remove-scope` runs as the account the uninstaller runs as, with a
reading that fails the next time the guide and the script disagree (review items 28 and 29).

## What works now

- With `wixen-mail.exe` already gone, the uninstall's message says "Your mail and settings may
  still be on this computer", and to delete the folder if it is still there. The Credential
  Manager paragraph and the sentence about a folder kept elsewhere are unchanged.
- The message after an uninstall for everybody says another account "may still have its own
  copy".
- The comments over `TheProgramIsAlreadyGone`, over the all-users message and over
  `[UninstallDelete]` say what Inno's source says: `RecordUninstallDeleteEntries` fills in
  `{localappdata}` when setup writes the uninstall log, so the folder removed is that of the
  account setup ran as. `docs/installing.md` gets the same hedge.
- `search-handler/README.md`, shipped as `windows-search.md`, says both commands run as the
  account the uninstaller runs as, what that leaves after an uninstall for everybody with
  another account's password, and where the message says how to untick the rule.
- `test_the_search_guide_says_who_runs_remove_scope_as_the_script_does` reads the account from
  the call carrying `'remove-scope'` in `CurUninstallStepChanged` (`Exec` or
  `ExecAsOriginalUser`, comments ignored) and from the guide's paragraph opening
  "Uninstalling", and requires them to agree. Its companion plants both wrong pairs, a guide
  naming neither (cannot tell, not a pass) and a call it cannot place.
