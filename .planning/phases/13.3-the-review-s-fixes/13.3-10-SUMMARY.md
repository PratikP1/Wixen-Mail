# Phase 13.3 Plan 10: A CardDAV answer cut short removes nobody Summary

A CardDAV answer the reader cannot vouch for as whole (a 507 stop, a card without its text, or
responses in a spelling the `d:` split never sees) now takes nobody off this computer, and a
contact an address book deleted while a change waited here goes with the change and is said,
instead of being refused on every sync.

## What changed

- `src/service/carddav.rs`: `CardsFromAServer` gains `stopped_short` and `is_whole()`.
  `cards_in` sets `stopped_short` for a block whose status is 507 (RFC 6352 section 8.6),
  passes over the address book's own block, and counts any other block without card text as a
  card that could not be read. After the blocks, `elements_named(xml, "response")` counts the
  response elements in any prefix and the difference from the blocks `response_blocks` split
  is added to `could_not_be_read`. `names_an_address_book` now calls the same counter.
- `src/application/carddav_sync.rs`: the removal pass runs when `arrived.is_whole()`; a
  stopped answer pushes "The server for {book} sent only part of the address book, so no
  contact was taken off this computer." into `errors` (logged and counted, names no contact).
- `src/application/contacts_sync.rs`: `take_off_who_a_whole_answer_left_out` drops the
  `the_copy_here_holds_work_nobody_has_sent(&local)` exception and keeps the choice hold; its
  doc says why. `one_address_book_deleted_them` then takes her off, or takes this address book
  off her where another still holds her, and `say_if_a_change_went_too` notes her for the
  existing sentence "A contact you had changed was deleted in your address book, and your
  change went with it".

## Deviations from Plan

1. **[Rule 2] Four new records, not two.** The plan named the 507 record and the response
   count. The block-without-text count and the address book's own block are the two halves
   of the line item 12 turns on, and nothing else guarded them, so each has a record measured
   by hand.
2. **[Rule 3] `a_marker_from_the_last_run` gives each provider's row its own id**
   (`state-{provider}`), because the five push tests store a marker for Google and for
   Outlook, and two rows with the id `state-1` collide on the primary key. A helper,
   `both_address_books_were_read_before`, stores both. No guard record anchored there.
3. **[Rule 1] Three more push tests read whole an address book naming nobody.**
   `test_a_refused_change_is_reported_as_a_setting_rather_than_as_an_error` and
   `test_an_edit_to_a_contact_both_address_books_know_reaches_both` now redden under "a
   deletion by one address book leaves a person the other still has", and
   `test_the_other_address_book_setting_is_named_only_while_the_change_is_somebodys_work`
   under the choice record, because their contact carries a change and is no longer kept out
   of the pass. They pass on the green; they were added to those records' red lists, each with
   a comment, rather than given markers, and both records were measured again.
4. **[Tooling] A re-measure left a break in the tree.** The first nineteen-record run reported
   "this record could not be measured: OSError(22, 'Invalid argument')" on "a deletion asks
   Google as well as Outlook what is owed" and kept going with that break still in
   `contacts_sync.rs`, so every later record was measured against two breaks. The run was
   stopped, the line put back by Edit, and the nineteen run again cleanly. Ledger 927.
5. **[Finding] Ledger 928, a question for Pratik**: after an answer that is not whole the
   sync still stores the address book's new change marker, so the cards past a server's limit
   are not asked for again until something changes. Not changed here.
6. **[Plan] Two ledger rows opened** (927, 928) where the plan said none; the header counts
   moved from 777 open and 926 in all to 779 and 928.
7. **[Brief] One read-only command began with `sed`**, a `sed -n '1,0p'` tail on a grep. The
   project hook refused it before it ran; nothing was read or written by it.
