# Phase 13.3 Plan 15: The Google sign-in asks who signed in through openid and email, and compares the Username

Both Google browser sign-ins now ask for `openid` and `email`, and the check reads the address from
Google's userinfo answer rather than the primary calendar, so a person who unticks Calendar keeps the
sign-in. Both are asked for, compared with and named as the account's Username, so a Gmail account
whose address box holds a send-as address on another domain is kept. Proven against loopback
stand-ins only; nothing has met a real Google account (ledger 931).

## What works now

- **Item 20.** `GoogleApiClient::the_address_signed_in` sends `GET {sign_in_base}/userinfo` with the
  token as a bearer and answers the `email` field trimmed; a 403, a blank `email` and an answer with
  none are each an error. `sign_in_base` is `https://openidconnect.googleapis.com/v1` from `new` and
  `for_account`, and `pointed_at` moves it with the other two bases. `GooglePrimaryCalendar` is gone
  (`git grep -n GooglePrimaryCalendar -- src` exits 1). Both Google scope lists begin with `openid`
  and end with `email`; the separate list still asks for no mail.
- **Item 19.** `Account::signs_in_as` answers the Username trimmed, or the address when it is blank.
  `sign_in_for_calendars` and `run_oauth_flow` call `.authorize(account.signs_in_as())`
  (wx_account_manager.rs:844 and :3256), so the login hint and the comparison name the Username;
  Outlook's hint follows. `what_the_separate_sign_in_does` and `what_sign_in_again_says_as_it_begins`
  name it, and `git grep -n "choose_in_the_browser(&account\.email)\|address: account\.email" --
  src/application/who_holds_the_calendars.rs` exits 1.
- **The refusal** reads: "Google signed in as someone@gmail.com, and this account's Username is
  me@gmail.com, so nothing was kept and nothing from someone@gmail.com was brought here. Sign in
  again, and choose me@gmail.com in the browser." It passes `reads_as_a_persons_sentence`.

## Deviations from Plan

1. **[Rule 1] `openid` goes first, not last.** The plan appended both permissions at the end of
   each list to keep a guard anchor in place, while its own premise 2 quoted Google's OpenID
   Connect page saying the scope "must begin with the openid value". Re-read on 2026-10-21 (page
   last updated 2026-06-15), the sentence stands. A real sign-in is the only test of whether Google
   enforces the order, and getting it wrong would fail the first sitting, so a third red and green
   pair puts `openid` first and `email` last; two records were rewritten for it and four
   re-measured.
2. **[Rule 3] No borrow in the two calls.** The plan's grep reads
   `.authorize(&account.signs_in_as())`; `signs_in_as` already answers `&str`, so the borrow is one
   clippy refuses under `-D warnings`. The calls and the reading use `.authorize(account.signs_in_as())`.
3. **[Hook] Task 2's first red commit was refused** because its trailer spelled the count check
   `house_style::test_every_...`; cargo reports an integration test by its bare name. The same
   commit then passed with the bare name.
4. **[Shape] The second google_api case gained an ordinary row.** A blank `email`, a missing one and
   a 403 are all errors under the old code too, so a case of only those was green against it; a
   fourth row with a real address makes it red against the calendar question and is the row that
   proves the trim.
5. **[Rule 2] Help's "Calendars arrive and contacts or tasks do not" bullet** also names an unticked
   box on Google's screen, and the privacy page says the address Google gives is compared and never
   written down, read in `sign_in_for_calendars_selected` and Sign In Again's failure arm.
6. **[Count] 23 records flagged, not 22.** The new record was among those the count check named; it
   was passed twice in the one call and agreed both times.
